# Code Of Conduct

This project is built for **WarriorHacks 2.0** (https://warriorhacks-2-0.devpost.com/), a student-led online hackathon run by the Westwood Computer Science Club in Austin, Texas. Everyone who contributes to this repository, including teammates, reviewers, testers, and anyone opening an issue or pull request, agrees to follow two codes:

1. **The official WarriorHacks 2.0 Code of Conduct**, which is binding for the event:
   https://docs.google.com/document/d/1g-o21ab84v7vmqpqpRk_RLHb8hrAFNw52n1-SQg9Eds/edit
2. **This repository's code**, below, which adds project-specific expectations. Where the two differ, the stricter rule applies.

## Our Values

Respect, inclusion, safety, learning and integrity. These are the event's values and ours.

## Our Pledge

We pledge to make participation in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation. We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive and healthy community.

## Expected Behavior

- Be kind, patient and supportive, especially with beginners and teammates new to a tool.
- Use clear, inclusive language. Assume good intent. Give and accept constructive feedback gracefully.
- Keep all communication, code, commit messages, test data and demo content appropriate for a school setting.
- Ask for consent before recording anyone, quoting private messages, or adding someone to a group chat.
- Credit teammates, libraries, datasets, models and any pre-existing code.
- Follow all applicable laws, school rules and platform terms (GitHub, Devpost, Vercel, every API provider).

## Unacceptable Behavior

- Harassment or discrimination of any kind, including based on any protected characteristic.
- Threats, stalking, doxxing, intimidation or sustained disruption.
- Sexualized language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Hate speech, slurs, or violent or NSFW content anywhere in the repository, issues, demo data or media.
- Sharing anyone's private information without explicit consent.
- Spam, scams, impersonation or phishing.
- Cheating, plagiarism, misrepresenting work, or tampering with another team's project.

## Youth Safety

Many WarriorHacks participants are minors. We follow the event's youth-safety rules:

- No one-on-one private messaging between adults and minors. Use shared team channels, or include another organizer or mentor.
- No private calls or breakout rooms with a single adult and a minor.
- Never put a real minor's name, photo, contact details, school schedule or health information into this repository, the demo, test fixtures or screenshots. Use clearly fictional data.

## Project Integrity (Hackathon Rules)

- **Everything in this repository's product code is created during the event** (after the theme reveal on Sep 27, 2026). Planning documents in `docs/` are preparation only.
- Any open-source code, previous personal code, third-party library, dataset, model or UI kit is disclosed and attributed in the README.
- **AI tools are allowed with disclosure.** Every AI tool used to write code or content (for example Claude, TypeSafe Jev, v0, DevSwarm agents) is listed in the README's "AI Use Disclosure" section.
- The submitted project must be a working system, not generated screenshots or text.
- Demos are honest: no faked features, no pre-recorded results presented as live, no hidden hard-coding presented as intelligence. Seeded demo data is labeled as demo data.

## Safety-Critical Honesty

This project gives heat and air-quality guidance for outdoor activity. Contributors must never:

- Weaken a safety threshold, remove a disclaimer, or make the app appear more certain than it is in order to make a demo look better.
- Present the app as a medical device, a diagnosis tool, or a replacement for an athletic trainer, an on-site WBGT instrument, or emergency services.

## Scope

This code applies in the repository (code, issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages), in team channels, in the live demo and in any public communication about the project, from pre-event planning through post-event follow-up.

## Reporting

- **Inside the team:** tell the team lead, or any teammate you trust, in writing.
- **To the event:** email **wwhscomputerscience@gmail.com** or message a WarriorHacks organizer in the event Discord. Include what happened, who was involved, where and when, and any screenshots or links.
- **Security issues** follow [SECURITY.md](./SECURITY.md), not this process.

Reports are handled as privately as possible, and the safety of the person reporting comes first. Retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith is itself a violation.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity, responses range from a private conversation or written warning, to removal of content, to removal from the team or repository. Violations of the event code are reported to the WarriorHacks organizers, whose own enforcement ladder includes disqualification and removal from the event.
