## 6. Rules And Code Of Conduct

The 2.0 Code of Conduct (Google Doc linked from the rules page) is **word for word the same** as the 2025 document apart from the event name and dates [V, diffed]. Key clauses:

### 6.1 Project integrity (these affect how we work) [V]

- "Your submission must be created during the event. You may use open-source code, previous personal code, and third-party libraries, but disclose what existed before and attribute it."
- "Don't submit others' work as your own. Any significant borrowed assets (code, models, datasets, UI kits) must be clearly cited."
- "If your project uses user data, do so lawfully and transparently; obtain consent; avoid collecting personally identifiable information (PII) unless essential and secured."
- The 2025 rules FAQ also said: "Any digital prototype aligned with the theme revealed on Sept 26. You may **not** submit projects you worked on before the theme reveal." [V, 2025 rules page]. The 2.0 rules page no longer shows this FAQ, but the CoC line "must be created during the event" carries the same meaning. **We treat "no product code before the theme reveal" as binding.**

### 6.2 AI usage [V]

- "AI tools (code assistants, model APIs, generative media) are allowed with disclosure."
- "Your final project must be a working system; not just generated screenshots or text."
- "Verify licenses for any generated assets/models and comply with provider policies."

**What this means for us:** planning documents (this folder) are allowed preparation, the same as reading documentation. **No application code, no scaffolding and no repo commits of product code before Sep 27.** Every AI tool we use (Claude, Jev, v0 if used, DevSwarm agents) is disclosed in the README and in the Devpost story.

### 6.3 Behavior, youth safety, privacy [V]

- Values: Respect, Inclusion, Safety, Learning, Integrity.
- Unacceptable: harassment, discrimination, threats, doxxing, sexualized content, hate speech, sharing private info, spam, scams, impersonation, phishing, "Cheating, plagiarism, misrepresentation of work, or tampering with others' projects."
- Youth safety: "No one-on-one private messaging between adults and minors; use public channels or include another organizer/mentor (two-adult rule)." No private breakout rooms with a single adult.
- Media: sessions may be recorded; media consent is opt in or out at registration.
- Privacy: participant contact info is for event communication only.
- Accessibility: accommodation requests to wwhscomputerscience@gmail.com, ideally 3+ days ahead.
- Reporting: email or DM an organizer with name, what happened, who, where and when, and screenshots.
- Enforcement ladder: warning, removal from a channel, **disqualification of a submission**, removal from the event with no awards and notification of school or guardians, contacting platform security or law enforcement.
- Scope: pre-event onboarding, the full window and post-event communications.

### 6.4 Devpost platform rules that also apply [V]

- Devpost Terms of Service and Community Guidelines (links on every page).
- Video must be a YouTube, Vimeo or Youku link. Private videos fail; unlisted works.
- The submission can be edited until the deadline; after the deadline, edits do not reach the judged version.

## 7. Judging

### 7.1 Criteria, verbatim [V]

| Criterion | Devpost text | Organizer site gloss |
|---|---|---|
| **Impact** | "Addresses a meaningful problem; clear audience & value." | "Does it solve a real problem worth solving? Who does it help?" |
| **Feasibility** | "Technically achievable and sustainable." | "Could this really get built, or does it already work? Is the plan believable?" |
| **User Experience** | "Accessible, intuitive, and user-friendly." | "Is it clear, usable, and considered? Does the design serve the user?" |
| **Technical Craft** | "Architecture, code quality, originality, innovation." | "How well is it made? Ambition, execution, and attention to detail." |

Site header for the section: "Scoring Process. Judges look for projects that solve something real and feel good to use." [V]

Weights: the 2025 rules listed **25% each** [V]. The 2.0 pages list the same four criteria without weights; we assume equal weights [I].

### 7.2 How Devpost judging works [V, help.devpost.com]

- Organizers configure up to 6 criteria. Judges rate each criterion **1 to 5 stars**; with weights on, the overall is a weighted average.
- Judges use a private judging dashboard, go submission by submission, can recuse, and can revisit scores before finalizing. Comments are optional.
- Winners are announced on Devpost with ribbons on the gallery.

**Implication [I]:** with 1 to 5 stars on four equal criteria, ties are common and one weak criterion drops a project out of the top 3. A balanced 5/5/5/5 beats a lopsided 5/5/5/2. Judges usually watch the video first and then open the live link and the repo.

### 7.3 The judges [V names, I inferences]

Listed on Devpost with no titles: **Prayash Gyawali, Nischal Kotamraju, Aditya Bhadra, Aarav Karumanchi, Leif Ramirez, Lucas Janssen, Arun Arunachalam.**

**What this tells us [I]:** the 2.0 panel is mostly technically literate students who have built Next.js and AI apps themselves and who know what won in 2025. They will open the GitHub repository, they will recognize a thin wrapper, and they will reward polish, a relatable problem and a real working flow.

## 10. Devpost Mechanics

### 10.1 Registration and teams [V, help.devpost.com]

- Register for the hackathon before you can see or start a submission (article 119).
- Participants page: set "looking for teammates" with an intro. A team-up request emails the other person, and **you can message each user only once** (article 75).
- The team leader creates the project and invites members with a link or by email; each invitee accepts (article 121). One project page per team.

### 10.2 Submission form [V, article 126]

1. **Manage team.**
2. **Project overview:** Project name, Project tagline (about 200 characters max; the longest tagline among 124 recent winners is exactly 200), Thumbnail (JPG, PNG or GIF, max 5 MB, 3:2 recommended).
3. **Project details:** the Project Story with default headings: *Inspiration, What it does, How we built it, Challenges we ran into, Accomplishments that we're proud of, What we learned, What's next for <Project>*. Markdown supported (headings, lists, tables, code, images) and LaTeX. **Built With** tags (max 25). **Try it out** links (live demo, GitHub). Image gallery. **Video demo link.**
4. **Additional info:** hackathon-specific questions (typically track selection and repo URL; file upload up to 35 MB).
5. **Submit:** accept terms; the green bar confirms and the project shows "Submitted".

### 10.3 Video rules [V, article 85]

- Must be a YouTube, Vimeo or Youku URL ("Must be a valid YouTube, Vimeo, or Youku url"). Google Drive does not embed.
- Unlisted is fine; private is not. On YouTube choose "Not made for kids".
- Processing can take minutes to hours. Upload early.
- The event requires 2 to 3 minutes. Over-length videos are a common disqualification reason on Devpost [V, Devpost blog].

### 10.4 Editing and freeze [V, article 123]

- Edit freely until the deadline. After it, portfolio edits "will not be reflected in your hackathon submission".
- The live site and repo are external. Judges may treat post-deadline commits as a rules issue [I]. **We tag a release (`v1.0.0-warriorhacks`) at submission time and do not push to `main` again until winners are announced.**

## 12. Competitive Landscape

### 12.1 Expected field size [I]

- 461 to 466 registered (vs 120 in 2025). 2025 had a submit rate of about 38% (46 of 120 Devpost registrants; organizers counted 128 participants).
- Estimate: **150 to 200 submissions across both tracks**, perhaps 100 to 140 in the Hackathon track. Three placements per track.

### 12.2 What wins comparable student hackathons [V data from 17 events and 124 winning projects, I patterns]

- AI is table stakes: about **73% of all winners and 78% of top-3 placements** were AI-powered. What wins is **"AI plus something"**: on-device models, computer vision, speech, retrieval over real documents, an explainable algorithm. Thin chatbots end up as honorable mentions.
- Top-3 categories: health or medical (~13), education (~11), climate or sustainability (~9), civic, legal or disaster safety (~8), accessibility as primary (~6, and a secondary frame in many more), mental health (~4).
- About 57% of top-3 projects had a live, non-GitHub demo link (Vercel dominant).
- 2025 to 2026 stack trend: Next.js + Tailwind + shadcn/ui + Supabase or Firebase + Vercel. AI coding tools are openly listed in Built With and not penalized.
- Common reasons projects lose: judges cannot tell what it is in the first 30 seconds; no working end-to-end path; scope creep; slick landing page with a thin repo; generic problem with no named user; chatbot wrapper; missing or broken deliverables (private video, over 3 minutes, dead link); poor audio; unbalanced criteria; off-theme or pre-built work.

### 12.3 Ideas judges have seen too often [V, Devpost search counts on 2026-09-25]

"mental health" 13,515; "food waste" 4,925; "AI tutor" 3,921; "carbon footprint" 3,940; "fact check" 3,847; "recycling" 3,731; "flashcards" 2,740; "financial literacy" 2,727; "mental health chatbot" 2,102; "loneliness" 1,783; "scam detection" 1,136; "study buddy" 1,085. See [problem.md](./problem.md).

### 12.4 Theme prediction [I, speculative]

- 1.0's theme was a problem-framed social-impact sentence: *"Build a tool that breaks down barriers to X, making Y more inclusive, accessible, and impactful."* Expect a similar one-sentence prompt.
- The tag change from "Social Good" to **"Open Ended"**, the addition of games to the tagline and the no-code Ideathon suggest a **broader, more open** theme.
- Regional trend in 2026: short slogans such as "Tech for Humanity" (Katy Youth Hacks 2026), "Small Problem. Real Impact." (Cosmo Hacks), "a more efficient way to live" (Triple H Houston), "Into the Skies" (hackPHS 2026).
- Most likely families: people-centric impact (community, connection, accessibility, "a real problem for a real group"), wellbeing and health, sustainability and climate, efficiency or everyday life, education (home turf, less likely to repeat verbatim).
