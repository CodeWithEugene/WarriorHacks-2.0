01 · At A Glance

The short version

The issue

From August 1, 2026, Texas UIL requires a wet bulb globe temperature (WBGT) reading before and every 30 minutes during every outdoor practice and marching band rehearsal, with limits on time, breaks and football gear at each flag level. Coaches and band directors, not trainers, usually run it. Paid systems cost hundreds to thousands of dollars per site. Exertional heat stroke is the leading preventable cause of death in high school football.

What we built

Flagline, a free, bilingual (English and Spanish) web app with no hardware and no accounts. It shows the flag now and for the next 7 days, answers plain-language plans, runs Practice Mode with timers and a meter photo reader, routes athlete check-ins with red-flag alerts, guides the heat stroke protocol and logs everything.

Why it is trustworthy

The safety core is deterministic code: a real physics model (Liljegren WBGT) checked against 288 reference cases, UIL thresholds stored as versioned data with a conformance suite, 442 automated tests. AI only interprets language and photos, gated on confidence. It never sets a flag level, a limit, or clears an athlete.

Status

Live in production at flagline.codewitheugene.top with a fictional demo team. Every feature in this dossier marked built works today on the live site. Open source under MIT.

02 · The Problem

A new rule, a deadly risk,
and no one to run it

Every afternoon from August to October, someone decides whether and how a team practices in dangerous heat. The right measure is WBGT, which combines air temperature, humidity, wind and sun. It is not the number on a weather app.

UIL, 2026-27: “The use of Wet Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT) to monitor environmental conditions and guide activity modifications is no longer a recommendation, but a required standard for all UIL outdoor athletic and marching band activities.” Readings are required within 15 minutes before practice and every 30 minutes during it.

The stakes

It is local

A 15-year-old Lancaster (Dallas area) player died of heat stroke in August 2025 after conditioning in a non-air-conditioned facility. An Austin Bowie High School player spent time in the ICU after heat stroke in a 2023 game. Band is at risk too: about 400 marching band members with exertional heat illness were reported in the news from 1990 to 2020, with no national band standard, and a university band member died after collapsing at a 91 F game on September 5, 2026.

It is national

A record 8,266,244 US high school students played sports in 2024-25 (NFHS). Texas joins at least 17 other states that require WBGT-guided practices, and states have adopted nearly 200 heat-illness prevention policies since 2017. Parents estimate their kids lost about a week of practices and games in 2024 to heat, smoke and other weather.

04 · The Solution

Five verbs: plan, prepare,
run, protect, prove

Plan
The WBGT flag for every practice hour for 7 days, and plain-language questions like “Can we go full pads 4 to 6 tomorrow?” answered with a rules check and better time windows. Quick Check works for any place: UIL rules in Texas, Korey Stringer Institute categories elsewhere.
Prepare
Exactly what the rules require at the expected level: maximum length, breaks per hour, football gear, conditioning, cooling zone, water. Borderline readings (within 1 F of the next level) are flagged so the stricter preparations are ready.
Run
Practice Mode: the pre-practice reading (keypad or a photo of the meter), a 30-minute recheck countdown, a break timer, time used against the level's limit, a cooling zone checklist, a thunder hold, and instant rule changes when the level rises. The coach and staff see the same practice, synced.
Protect
Athletes scan a QR code and say how they feel, in English or Spanish, with no account. Red flags (confusion, stopped sweating, fainting) alert the coach at once and open the heat stroke protocol: cool first, transport second, with a cooling timer and temperature log.
Prove
Every reading, level change, break and check-in is timestamped in a live log and exportable as CSV for the athletic director. Parents get a public team page.

Principles we did not bend

The human decides. The app never says “safe”. It shows the flag and what the rules require.
Deterministic where it matters. Heat math and rules are tested code; AI never decides a level.
Honest numbers. Every value shows its source and whether it is a forecast or a measurement.
The measured reading wins. Forecasts plan; on-site readings drive the live rules.
Five seconds on the sideline. Every live action is one or two taps, with 44 px targets.
Color is never the only signal. Every flag has an icon, a name and a value.
Bilingual from day one. English and Spanish everywhere, including check-ins.
Privacy by default. No athlete names needed; check-ins can be anonymous.

07 · The Heat Engine And The Rules

Real physics, the exact
UIL table

WBGT, computed

Outdoor WBGT = 0.7 × natural wet bulb + 0.2 × globe + 0.1 × air. We ported the Liljegren et al. (2008) model from ECMWF's open-source thermofeel library to TypeScript, including the globe and natural wet bulb solvers, 2 m wind adjustment and NOAA solar geometry. It matches 288 reference cases generated from the original implementation. On a late-September Austin afternoon: air 97 to 99 F, heat index 100 to 107 F, WBGT 85 to 88 F.

UIL WBGT activity guidelines, 2026-27

LevelClass 3 (most of Texas)Class 2 (Panhandle, South Plains)What the rules require
Green< 82.0< 79.7Normal activities; at least 3 rest breaks each hour, 3 minutes each
Yellow82.0 to 86.979.7 to 84.6Discretion for intense or prolonged exercise; 3 breaks of 4 minutes each hour; cooling zone required
Orange87.0 to 90.084.7 to 87.62 hours maximum; football in helmet, shoulder pads and shorts (pants allowed if reached mid-practice); 4 breaks of 4 minutes each hour
Red90.1 to 92.087.7 to 89.71 hour maximum; football: no protective equipment and no conditioning; 20 minutes of breaks across the hour
Black≥ 92.1≥ 89.8No outdoor workouts; delay until cooler

Timing rules we enforce

  • A reading within 15 minutes before practice and every 30 minutes during it (with a 2-minute grace).
  • When conditions worsen, everyone moves to the stricter level at once; when they improve, practice is not extended.
  • Water is never denied; breaks mean unlimited hydration and no activity.
  • Thunder or lightning starts a 30-minute hold that resets with each strike (UIL adopts NFHS).
  • Air quality presets: modify at AQI 101 to 150, cancel youth activity at 151+, with a stricter youth option.

Outside Texas

Korey Stringer Institute regional categories (Grundstein et al. 2015) apply anywhere else, with Category 1 as the most protective default.

08 · The AI Layer

Typed judgments,
not chatbot text

Instead of prompting a chatbot and parsing its words, Flagline asks TypeSafe Jev typed questions (Choice, Noul probabilities and Scores) with confidence values our code can gate on. AI handles only what code cannot: understanding people and photos.

Check-in triage

Jev screens free text for 8 red flags (confusion, collapse, vomiting, stopped sweating, severe headache, seizure, breathing, chest pain) plus severity and category, in one request.

Routing is recall-first and deterministic: any tapped red flag, an English or Spanish keyword, a red-flag probability ≥ 0.25, severity ≥ 3.0 or “possible heat stroke” goes to Emergency. AI can raise a flag, never clear one.

Ask Flagline

A date parser proposes start times and lengths (“4 to 6 tomorrow” means 4 PM; “about 2 hours” is a length). Jev picks intent, gear, conditioning and the right candidate in one request.

Below 0.6 confidence the app asks instead of guessing. The rules engine then evaluates the plan hour by hour and finds better windows.

Meter photo

A vision model (Kimi K3 via OpenRouter) only transcribes the numbers and labels on the display. Jev picks which number is WBGT.

Code converts Celsius, rejects values outside 40 to 120 F, warns when more than 8 F from the forecast, and the coach confirms. Photos are read once and never stored.

09 · Safety, Privacy, Accessibility

Built for the worst
afternoon of the season

Hazards and controls

HazardControls
App shows a lower level than realityHigher of two forecasts; borderline flag within 1 F; forecast labeled planning only; on-site reading prompted before and every 30 minutes; the measured reading drives the rules
A rule encoded wronglyRules are versioned data with source links; conformance tests on every boundary; a threshold change needs a source and a passing test
Missed recheckBig countdown, overdue state, synced practice for staff, logged
An athlete's red flag not escalatedTapped red flags bypass AI; keyword backstop in two languages; recall-first thresholds; 100% recall eval; the app never clears a flag
Transport before coolingThe protocol leads with cooling; Call 911 after cooling starts; large type; bilingual
Over-trustNever says “safe”; the coach confirms; copy frames the app as support for school staff
Misread meter photoRange and forecast checks; the coach confirms; manual entry always available

Privacy

  • No athlete names required; jersey numbers or initials are optional; check-ins can be anonymous.
  • No accounts; coach links stored only as hashes.
  • Meter photos processed in memory and never stored.
  • Public team pages hold no personal data.
  • All demo data is fictional (Pecan Creek High School).
  • AI keys stay on the server.

Accessibility

  • Every flag is color plus icon plus name plus value.
  • 44 px targets in Practice Mode; one-handed layouts.
  • Light, dark and a sunlight theme for glare.
  • Labels match visible text (WCAG 2.5.3); selected states never rely on shade alone.
  • English and Spanish on every screen, enforced by a test that checks every message key.

Flagline is not a medical device and does not diagnose illness. It does not replace an athletic trainer, an on-site WBGT instrument or emergency services. Decisions remain with school staff under the UIL heat plan and the school's emergency action plan.
